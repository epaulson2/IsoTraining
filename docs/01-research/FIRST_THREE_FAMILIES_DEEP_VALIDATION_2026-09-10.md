# Deep Validation — First Three Extreme-Iso Families

Date: 2026-09-10

## Purpose

This document records the deeper technical validation behind the first three exercise-family drafts in the IsoTraining project: split stance / lunge, horizontal push / chest, and vertical pull / scapular-lat. The goal is to separate high-confidence historical Schroeder/EVO cues from later coaching interpretations and from our own evidence-based extensions.

## Source confidence model

### Level A — Primary / direct Schroeder
Use when Jay Schroeder is directly quoted or clearly speaking about the movement.

### Level B — First-generation or direct EVO trainee / coach documentation
Use when the source reports having trained under EVO / Schroeder or gives a detailed exercise appendix derived from that lineage.

### Level C — Later derivative interpretation
Useful for cross-checking, but not sufficient by itself to label a cue as Schroeder-original.

## Global Schroeder principles validated

1. An Iso-Extreme is not intended to be a passive static hold. Schroeder describes beginning in the deepest acceptable position and continually attempting to move farther into that position while maintaining the required geometry.
2. Direction matters. Historical descriptions repeatedly use the concept of actively pulling in the direction the body is trying to move rather than simply bracing against gravity.
3. The long muscle should remain active rather than simply being passively stretched.
4. Posture is part of the exercise. Losing the prescribed geometry changes the exercise.
5. Duration is not the only progression variable. As the athlete learns to create more active force, a shorter hold can represent a harder effort than a longer passive one.

## Family 1 — Split Stance / Lunge

### High-confidence historical geometry

The best detailed coaching appendix found gives the following lunge structure:

- long split stance;
- front heel slightly elevated in that specific coaching version;
- front lower leg perpendicular to the ground;
- front thigh approximately parallel to the ground;
- rear leg placed as far back as controllably possible;
- torso and head tall / proud rather than folded forward;
- arms relaxed at the sides in the classic drill.

Important: the elevated front heel should be treated as a historical variation, not a universal mandatory rule, because other EVO-derived accounts also describe flat-ground, front-foot-elevated and rear-foot-elevated versions.

### High-confidence internal intent

The most consistent cue set is:

- maximally contract the front hamstring;
- attempt to drag the front foot backward without letting it move;
- maximally contract the rear glute;
- drive the rear foot backward into the ground and/or attempt to bring the rear thigh forward;
- think of the two legs as scissors trying to close toward one another.

A direct EVO trainee independently described the lead leg as trying to perform a leg curl through the hamstrings while the rear leg maintains tension pulling backward and upward. This strongly corroborates the front-hamstring / rear-glute emphasis.

### What should not be over-specified

- Exact knee angle should not be reduced to a rigid 90-degree rule for every person.
- Front heel elevation is not required for every variation.
- The rear knee does not need to touch the floor.
- A perfectly vertical torso may be a useful historical cue but should not be forced at the expense of joint comfort or the intended muscle actions.

### Updated teaching implication

Learn Mode should teach the lunge in this order:

1. long split stance;
2. front-shin orientation;
3. torso / pelvis control;
4. front-hamstring drag-back intent;
5. rear-glute / rear-leg drive intent;
6. scissor the legs together internally;
7. only then add depth and duration.

This is substantially better than teaching the lunge as a generic split-squat hold.

## Family 2 — Horizontal Push / Chest

### High-confidence historical geometry

Historical Schroeder-lineage material repeatedly emphasizes:

- plank-like whole-body organization;
- forearms perpendicular to the floor in the bottom position;
- deep chest position, often using blocks / chairs to permit greater range;
- elbow angle is less important than forearm-to-floor orientation;
- the athlete should descend only as far as active shoulder and trunk control can be maintained.

Later detailed coaching material often uses elbows roughly 45 degrees from the torso, but this should be treated as a practical default rather than an inviolable Schroeder rule.

### High-confidence internal intent

The defining historical idea is that the athlete is not merely trying to press upward. Instead:

- actively pull deeper into the push-up;
- use posterior musculature to pull the body toward the floor;
- think of a rowing action through the biceps / upper back;
- simultaneously maintain anterior-chain activity so the chest and shoulder musculature lengthen under active control rather than relaxing passively.

This creates the characteristic co-contraction: posterior musculature pulls the athlete down while anterior musculature resists and controls the lengthening.

### Important correction to the earlier draft

The earlier wording "actively create horizontal pressing tension" is incomplete and potentially misleading. The primary Schroeder-style learning cue should be **pull down while resisting that pull**, not simply "press hard while holding the bottom."

### Standing push-off

Historical accounts consistently identify the standing doorway push-up / push-off as a foundational position and useful regression. The same principle applies: actively pull toward the wall / doorway while maintaining opposing muscular control.

### Strap chest press

The strap press remains an **Evidence-Based Extension**, not a Schroeder-original drill. It should be programmed as a targeted overcoming isometric, complementary to the integrated push-up. It should not replace the push-up in the core curriculum because it removes much of the whole-body stabilization demand.

## Family 3 — Vertical Pull / Scapular-Lat

### What is strongly supported

The scapular pull-up is consistently listed among Schroeder's core Iso-Extreme positions. Direct EVO trainee documentation also confirms it was one of the first four movements prescribed.

The historical principle remains the same: active muscular control in an overhead position rather than a completely passive dead hang.

### What is not yet strongly supported

Compared with the lunge and push-up, the exact historical geometry is less well documented. We do not currently have sufficiently strong primary evidence to prescribe all of the following as rigid Schroeder rules:

- a specific grip width;
- a fixed degree of scapular depression;
- maximal scapular retraction;
- a particular elbow bend;
- a single canonical rib-cage position.

Therefore the family spec should remain conservative.

### Best current technical interpretation

Teach the trainee to distinguish:

1. passive supported hang;
2. lightly active shoulder organization;
3. lat / scapular tension while keeping elbows nearly straight;
4. controlled transition toward a longer overhead position without losing muscular support.

The exercise should not be taught as "depress the shoulder blades as hard as possible for as long as possible." That may shorten the lats and defeat the long-position goal. The better working model is active support plus controlled length, with the athlete learning how much scapular motion preserves muscular tension and shoulder comfort.

### Strap pulldown / straight-arm pull

This remains an **Evidence-Based Extension**. It is useful precisely because it lets us train lat intent in a more controllable way than a full bodyweight hang, but it should not be mislabeled as Schroeder-original.

## Programming implications from the validation pass

### Lunge
The key learning target is not time. It is the ability to generate the front-hamstring / rear-glute scissor action without losing geometry.

### Push-up
The key learning target is active descent intent plus anterior-chain control. A shallower position with true co-contraction is preferable to a deeper position in which the athlete simply rests on end range.

### Scapular pull-up
The key learning target is active overhead control. Do not force maximal depression if it turns the exercise into a shortened-lat hold.

## Current conclusion

The lunge and push-up can now be upgraded from provisional to **technically validated draft** status. The scapular-pull-up remains **partially validated**: its historical inclusion is secure, but its exact scapular geometry requires more direct source material before publication-level claims are made.
