# IsoTraining Beginner 8-Week Program

## Status

**Phase 3 — locked beginner program draft.**

This program implements the locked IsoTraining programming architecture. It is designed for a healthy beginner who has not yet developed proficiency in the Extreme-Iso positions.

The program is intentionally conservative. It prioritizes position quality, active intent, reproducible setup, and gradual accumulation of work before higher-force targeted isometrics are emphasized.

## Program goals

By the end of 8 weeks, the trainee should be able to:

- independently set up the major Tier A positions;
- distinguish active Extreme-Iso intent from passive holding;
- maintain clean geometry for 20–30 second main-position bouts;
- accumulate roughly 60–90 seconds of quality work on major positions;
- perform selected strap isometrics at approximately 6–7/10 effort without posture change;
- exit all positions under control;
- recognize technical failure before absolute muscular failure;
- be ready for intermediate programming if the graduation criteria are met.

## Weekly schedule

Train on 3 nonconsecutive days each week.

Suggested rhythm:

- **Day A — Monday**
- **Day B — Wednesday**
- **Day C — Friday**

Equivalent spacing is acceptable.

Do not perform hard targeted work for the same tissue on consecutive days.

## Warm-up

Before each session:

1. 3–5 minutes of easy general movement.
2. One easy dynamic rehearsal of each movement pattern being trained.
3. For any exercise not yet mastered, perform 1–2 Learn Mode rehearsals of 6–10 seconds at 3–4/10 effort before the work sets.

The warm-up should improve position awareness, not create fatigue.

# Session templates

## Day A — Foundational integrated emphasis

1. **Integrated Extreme Lunge**
2. **Extreme Push-Up**
3. **Standing Glute-Ham / Standing Hamstring Iso**
4. **Body-Supported Row Iso**
5. **Straight-Knee Calf Iso**

Optional only after Week 4 and if recovery is good:
6. **Strap External-Rotation Iso**

## Day B — Squat + vertical/scapular emphasis

1. **Iso-Extreme Wall Squat**
2. **Scapular Pull-Up / Active Hang Extreme Iso**
3. **Supported Lateral-Lunge / Cossack Iso**
4. **Strap Extreme Chest Press**
5. **Bent-Knee Calf Iso**

Optional only after Week 4 and if technique is solid:
6. **Preacher Curl Extreme Iso**

## Day C — Mixed full-body integration

1. **Integrated Extreme Lunge**
2. **Standing Glute-Ham / Standing Hamstring Iso**
3. **Extreme Push-Up**
4. **Body-Supported Row Iso**
5. **Iso-Extreme Wall Squat**

Optional from Week 5 onward:
6. **Strap Overhead Triceps Iso**

## Why the week is organized this way

The beginner program deliberately repeats the highest-value integrated positions often enough to build skill. It does not attempt to expose the trainee to all 14 Tier A exercises equally.

The core repeated skills are:

- lunge;
- squat;
- hinge / hamstring;
- horizontal push;
- horizontal pull;
- active overhead / scapular control.

Calf, lateral-stance, chest-strap, external-rotation, biceps, and triceps work are supporting exposures.

# Weeks 1–2 — Learn the positions

## Objective

Learn setup, working depth, pressure distribution, muscular intent, and controlled exits.

Do **not** chase fatigue.

### Main integrated exercises

- **2 sets × 10–15 seconds**
- effort: **4–5/10**
- rest: **45–75 seconds**

### Scapular / active-hang work

- **2 sets × 8–12 seconds**
- use foot support if needed
- effort: **4–5/10**

### Calf / lateral-stance work

- **2 sets × 10–15 seconds**
- use hand support freely
- effort: **4–5/10**

### Strap Chest Press on Day B

Week 1:
- treat as Learn Mode only;
- **2 × 5 seconds at 4–5/10**.

Week 2:
- **2 × 6 seconds at 5/10**.

### Position-learning rule

If an exercise cannot be reproduced cleanly, replace its work sets with Learn Mode practice for that day.

### Progression from Week 1 to Week 2

Progress only if:

- setup was reproducible;
- no sharp joint symptoms occurred;
- target tension was felt where intended;
- the trainee exited under control.

If those conditions are met, add approximately **2–5 seconds per hold** in Week 2 rather than increasing effort.

---

# Week 3 — First build week

## Objective

Begin accumulating meaningful position time while keeping technical reserve.

### Main integrated exercises

- **3 sets × 15–20 seconds**
- effort: **5–6/10**
- accumulated time: **45–60 seconds**
- rest: **60–90 seconds**

### Scapular active hang

- **3 × 10–15 seconds**
- partial bodyweight is acceptable
- effort: **5–6/10**

### Calf and lateral-stance positions

- **2–3 × 15–20 seconds**
- effort: **5–6/10**

### Strap Chest Press

- **3 × 6 seconds**
- effort: **5–6/10**
- rest: **60–90 seconds**

No additional targeted high-intent exercises yet unless the trainee is already technically proficient.

---

# Week 4 — Consolidation

## Objective

Keep the learned positions while reducing fatigue.

Reduce accumulated position-work time by approximately **30–40%** relative to Week 3.

### Main integrated exercises

- **2 sets × 15–20 seconds**
- effort: **5/10**

### Strap Chest Press

- **2 × 5–6 seconds**
- effort: **5/10**

### Rule

Do not test maximum hold times. Use this week to improve setup precision and reduce unnecessary tension.

At the end of Week 4, the trainee should be able to explain the three checkpoints for each main exercise:

1. **Visual:** what must the position look like?
2. **Tension:** where should muscular work be felt?
3. **Intent:** what internal action is being attempted?

---

# Week 5 — Second build begins

## Objective

Move toward the beginner target of 60–90 seconds accumulated quality work and introduce a small amount of targeted isometric training.

### Main integrated exercises

- **3 × 20 seconds**
- effort: **5–6/10**
- accumulated time: **60 seconds**

### Scapular active hang

- **3 × 12–20 seconds**
- effort: **5–6/10**

### Calf / lateral-stance work

- **3 × 15–20 seconds**
- effort: **5–6/10**

### Strap Chest Press

- **3 × 6–8 seconds**
- effort: **6/10**

### Optional targeted work

If the base program is being tolerated well:

- Day A: **Strap External Rotation — 2 × 6 seconds @ 5–6/10**
- Day C: **Strap Overhead Triceps — 2 × 6 seconds @ 5–6/10**

Do not add both if shoulder or elbow recovery is questionable.

---

# Week 6 — Build duration

## Objective

Increase position duration before significantly increasing effort.

### Main integrated exercises

- **3 × 20–25 seconds**
- effort: **6/10**
- accumulated time: **60–75 seconds**

### Scapular active hang

- **3 × 15–20 seconds**

### Calf / lateral stance

- **3 × 20 seconds**

### Strap Chest Press

- **3 × 8 seconds @ 6–7/10**

### Optional targeted work

- external rotation: **2–3 × 6 seconds @ 6/10**
- overhead triceps: **2–3 × 6 seconds @ 6/10**
- preacher curl on Day B: **2 × 8–10 seconds @ 5–6/10** if the setup is already learned

Only one or two targeted exercises should appear in a session.

---

# Week 7 — Highest beginner build week

## Objective

Reach the upper end of beginner volume while retaining technical reserve.

### Main integrated exercises

Choose one of the following based on technical quality:

**Option A — duration progression**
- **3 × 25–30 seconds**

or

**Option B — accumulated-time progression**
- **4 × 20 seconds**

Do not increase both bout duration and set count simultaneously.

Target:
- **75–90 seconds accumulated**
- effort: **6–7/10**

### Scapular active hang

- **3–4 × 15–20 seconds**
- use assistance as needed to preserve active shoulder control

### Strap Chest Press

- **3–4 × 6–8 seconds @ 6–7/10**

### Optional targeted work

- external rotation: **3 × 6 seconds @ 6–7/10**
- triceps: **3 × 6 seconds @ 6–7/10**
- preacher curl: **2–3 × 8–10 seconds @ 6/10**

No beginner needs 9–10/10 targeted efforts.

---

# Week 8 — Consolidate and reassess

## Objective

Reduce fatigue and determine whether intermediate programming is appropriate.

### Volume

Reduce Week 7 accumulated position time by approximately **35%**.

Typical prescription:

- **2 × 20–25 seconds** for main integrated positions
- effort: **5–6/10**

### Targeted exercises

- **2 × 6 seconds @ 5–6/10**

### No max testing

Do not perform all-out hold-duration tests or 10/10 strap efforts.

Readiness is judged by reproducibility and control, not by suffering tolerance.

---

# Exercise-specific beginner notes

## Extreme Lunge

Begin with hand support if balance distracts from the front-hamstring drag-back / rear-leg scissor intent. Remove support only when the internal action remains clear.

## Wall Squat

Do not convert this into a generic wall sit. Maintain the validated geometry and active deeper-pulling intent. Depth stops increasing when the intended alignment can no longer be preserved.

## Standing Hamstring

The exercise should remain active. Do not chase greater torso depth by turning it into a passive hamstring stretch.

## Extreme Push-Up

Use wall, incline, or knees-supported regressions as needed. The goal is the correct bottom-position geometry and active pulling/deepening intent, not proving full floor push-up strength.

## Scapular Pull-Up / Active Hang

Foot or box support is encouraged for beginners. Passive hanging is not the objective.

## Body-Supported Row

Adjust body angle before adding effort. The trunk should remain stable rather than leaning or twisting to manufacture pulling force.

## Calf Isos

Use support for balance. The heel may lower only as far as muscular control is maintained; do not bounce or hang passively into dorsiflexion.

## Lateral Lunge / Cossack

Use hand support freely. The priority is frontal-plane control and active adductor loading, not achieving a visually dramatic depth.

## Strap Chest Press

This is the main targeted high-intent teaching exercise in the beginner plan. Learn to increase force without changing the selected shoulder/elbow geometry.

## External Rotation

Keep effort moderate. This is a precision accessory, not a maximal-force contest.

## Overhead Triceps

Do not force extreme shoulder flexion or elbow flexion. Use only a long but comfortable position.

# Session stop rules

Stop a set if:

- defining geometry changes;
- active muscular tension becomes passive hanging;
- the intended force direction is lost;
- a joint repeatedly collapses or shifts;
- uncontrolled breath-holding appears;
- sharp pain, pinching, numbness, tingling, or sudden joint discomfort occurs;
- the trainee can only continue by shortening or deforming the position.

Technical failure ends the set before absolute muscular failure.

## Pain rule

Normal muscular effort, shaking, fatigue, and stretching sensation can occur.

Sharp pain, joint pinching, neurological symptoms, or rapidly escalating tendon pain are not target sensations.

---

# Progression rule

The trainee progresses a given exercise only after completing the current prescription cleanly in at least **two sessions**.

Progress one major variable at a time:

1. technique;
2. intent;
3. duration;
4. accumulated time;
5. effort;
6. leverage / loading.

Do not increase depth, duration, and effort simultaneously.

---

# Beginner graduation criteria

The trainee is ready to leave the beginner program when the following are true for the main patterns:

- independently reproduces the setup;
- maintains correct force intent without coaching;
- performs **20–30 second clean integrated holds**;
- accumulates approximately **60–90 seconds** of quality work where prescribed;
- performs selected strap efforts at approximately **7/10** without posture change;
- distinguishes muscular work from passive stretch or joint loading;
- exits positions under control;
- recovers normally between 3 weekly sessions.

If these criteria are not met, repeat Weeks 5–8 or remain on the relevant regression rather than automatically entering intermediate programming.

---

# What the beginner program intentionally does not include

- five-minute continuous holds;
- 9–10/10 maximal strap efforts;
- altitude drops;
- rebound / reactive work;
- Extreme Slow as a formal training method;
- aggressive external loading;
- advanced dip isometrics;
- advanced single-leg squat isometrics;
- large numbers of accessory exercises.

Those belong later, after technical competence is established.

---

# Evidence and design rationale

This program follows the project-wide principle that isometric adaptations depend on variables such as joint/muscle length, contraction intensity, intent, and training specificity. Longer-muscle-length training can be useful for hypertrophy and transfer, while higher-intensity contractions are particularly relevant to tendon adaptation. The beginner plan therefore emphasizes technically controlled position work first and introduces only modest high-intent targeted work later.

The plan also preserves Schroeder/EVO-inspired longer positional work without assuming that every historical physiological explanation is established exercise science.
