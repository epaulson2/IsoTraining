# IsoTraining Program-Level Architecture

## Status

**Locked framework for beginner, intermediate and advanced program design.**

This document translates the programming principles into level-specific architecture. It does not yet prescribe every exercise for every week.

## Beginner

### Primary objectives
- learn and reproduce Tier A positions;
- distinguish active Extreme-Iso intent from passive holding;
- tolerate three weekly sessions;
- build basic accumulated time without technical failure;
- learn 1–2 simple targeted strap isometrics.

### Weekly structure
- 3 full-body sessions per week;
- 4–6 exercises per session;
- mostly Tier A integrated positions;
- 1–2 targeted exercises per session at most;
- no reactive / rebound methods.

### Typical dosage
- integrated holds: 10–30 seconds per bout, 2–4 bouts;
- accumulated time: 30–90 seconds per exercise;
- targeted high-intent efforts: 5–8 seconds, 2–4 efforts, mostly 5–7/10 effort;
- rest: 30–120 seconds depending on exercise demand.

### Progression emphasis
Technique first, then duration, then accumulated time, then effort.

## Intermediate

### Primary objectives
- increase quality accumulated time;
- introduce selected Tier B movements;
- use integrated and targeted versions strategically;
- increase high-intent work toward 7–9/10;
- begin higher leverage / modest external load where appropriate.

### Weekly structure
- 3–4 sessions per week;
- full-body rotation or upper/lower split;
- 5–7 exercises per session;
- each major family receives roughly 2 meaningful weekly exposures;
- integrated and targeted variants may alternate across sessions.

### Typical dosage
- integrated holds: 20–45 seconds per bout, 2–4 bouts;
- accumulated time: 60–150 seconds per exercise;
- targeted high-intent efforts: 5–10 seconds, 3–5 efforts;
- some selected Schroeder-style positions may progress beyond 2 minutes accumulated time.

### Progression emphasis
Increase intent and leverage only after position quality is robust. Introduce Extreme Slow near the later intermediate stage if the athlete meets progression gates.

## Advanced

### Primary objectives
- develop high-level positional endurance and force intent;
- selectively use long accumulated Extreme-Iso work;
- integrate Extreme Slow;
- introduce controlled force-absorption and rebound methods;
- preserve movement quality under increased load and complexity.

### Weekly structure
- typically 4 sessions per week;
- organized around lower/upper or movement emphasis;
- not every family is trained heavily every week;
- one major method emphasis should dominate a block rather than attempting to peak duration, maximal intent and reactivity simultaneously.

### Typical dosage
- selected position holds: 30–60+ seconds per bout;
- selected Schroeder-style positions may accumulate 2–5 minutes;
- targeted high-intent efforts: 5–10 seconds, usually 3–5 high-quality efforts;
- longer rest for very high-intent or reactive work.

### Advanced methods
1. longer accumulated Extreme-Iso work;
2. leverage / loading progression;
3. Extreme Slow;
4. controlled force absorption;
5. rebound / reactive pairing;
6. task-specific expression.

### Important rule
Advancement is competency-based, not calendar-based. Completing an intermediate plan does not automatically qualify a trainee for reactive work.

## Default block rhythm

For plans longer than four weeks:

- Weeks 1–3: build
- Week 4: consolidate
- Weeks 5–7: build
- Week 8: consolidate / reassess

Consolidation reduces total accumulated time by roughly 30–40% and high-intent sets by about one third while preserving movement quality.

## Integrated-targeted sequencing options

### Option A — Alternate
Integrated version on Day 1, targeted version on Day 2.

### Option B — Complement
Integrated position first, targeted iso second.

### Option C — Specialize
One mode temporarily receives most of the volume while the other is maintained at low dose.

### Default by level
- Beginner: integrated dominant.
- Intermediate: balanced.
- Advanced: block-specific.

## Readiness checkpoints

### Beginner → Intermediate
- reproducible Tier A setups;
- 20–30 second clean main holds;
- roughly 60–90 seconds accumulated quality work;
- short strap efforts at ~7/10 without compensation;
- normal recovery across three weekly sessions.

### Intermediate → Advanced
- stable geometry under longer accumulated work;
- repeatable 8–9/10 targeted efforts;
- tolerance of higher leverage / moderate loading;
- competent use of Tier B exercises;
- recovery across four weekly sessions;
- technical readiness for Extreme Slow before reactive methods.

## What programming should not do

- do not chase five-minute holds as a universal standard;
- do not make every set maximal;
- do not add exercises just to make a program look advanced;
- do not replace integrated positions with strap work;
- do not force deeper range after active muscular control is lost;
- do not introduce reactive work merely because a certain week number has arrived.
