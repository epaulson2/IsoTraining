# Home Eccentric Overload — Core 6 Learning Guide

## Status

**Working learning guide — Core 6 home eccentric-overload system.**

This document defines the first-pass teaching architecture for a minimalist home eccentric-overload program built around six high-value full-body movement patterns:

1. Ring Chest Press Eccentric
2. Ring Row Eccentric
3. Supported Sissy-Squat Eccentric
4. Assisted Nordic Hamstring Eccentric
5. Ring Fallout / Ab Rollout Eccentric
6. Assisted Pull-Up Eccentric

The program is intentionally built around **adjustable leverage rather than external weights**. Rings, a pull-up bar, a sturdy chair/box, and a safe anchor allow the trainee to make an exercise easier or harder simply by changing body position.

The defining idea is:

> **Get into the start position with assistance. Then remove enough assistance or change leverage until the eccentric phase is harder than you can lift concentrically. Fight the descent every inch.**

This guide is deliberately conservative for beginners. True supramaximal eccentric work is an advanced loading method. A beginner should first learn the movement path and escape strategy in a controllable eccentric zone before deliberately seeking a load that cannot be stopped.

---

# 1. The central concept — every exercise is a spectrum

Each exercise in this system is not one fixed movement. It is a **leverage continuum**.

For example, a ring chest press can range from:

- nearly upright with the feet close to the anchor;
- to a modest forward lean;
- to a deep ring push-up;
- to feet-elevated work;
- to advanced unilateral or highly destabilized positions.

The same principle applies to rows, Nordics, pull-ups, rollouts, and supported sissy squats.

That means the exercise progression and the resistance adjustment are largely the same thing.

## Two separate variables

Do not confuse these:

### A. Leverage difficulty
How mechanically demanding the chosen body position is.

Examples:
- farther feet from the ring anchor;
- more horizontal torso;
- less assistance;
- longer lever arm;
- deeper range;
- greater unilateral bias.

### B. Eccentric overload intensity
How far beyond your current concentric capacity the chosen position is.

A highly trained person may use an advanced variation but intentionally keep it submaximal. A beginner may use a very upright-looking ring press yet still make it supramaximal for their present strength by adjusting the feet precisely.

The system therefore teaches two questions:

1. **Which leverage level should I explore?**
2. **Where inside that level is today's correct eccentric overload?**

---

# 2. The four-zone calibration system

Every trainee should learn this before performing hard eccentric work.

## Green — too easy for eccentric-overload work

You can:
- stop the movement at will;
- reverse it;
- perform the concentric from the working position;
- control the eccentric with plenty of reserve.

This is still useful for learning the movement.

## Blue — controlled eccentric training

You can:
- resist strongly;
- control the full path;
- perhaps stop briefly;
- probably cannot perform many clean concentric repetitions from the hardest point.

**Beginners should spend substantial time here.**

## Orange — supramaximal sweet spot

You are trying hard to stop the movement, but:
- you cannot truly hold the position indefinitely;
- you cannot reverse into a concentric rep from the hardest point;
- gravity/leverage forces a slow eccentric despite maximal or near-maximal resistance;
- the path remains technically clean;
- you retain a safe escape.

This is the target zone for true supramaximal eccentric work after the movement is mastered.

## Red — too heavy

The resistance wins too quickly.

Signs:
- immediate drop or acceleration;
- loss of joint position;
- inability to keep the intended movement path;
- panic or uncontrolled bailout;
- sharp pain or joint strain;
- the rep becomes a catch rather than a controlled eccentric.

**Red is not “more advanced.” Red is simply too much load.**

---

# 3. Universal execution standard

For all six exercises, use the same learning sequence.

## Step 1 — Learn the path with easy leverage

Before supramaximal loading, perform 2–4 easy practice reps so you know:
- where the movement begins;
- where it ends;
- which joints should move;
- which joints should remain stable;
- exactly how you will exit.

## Step 2 — Learn the assisted reset

The concentric return should usually be easier than the eccentric.

Examples:
- step forward before returning the rings;
- use both legs to rise before a one-leg eccentric;
- use a chair to return to the top of a pull-up;
- use the hands to reset a Nordic.

Do not turn the return into an accidental hard concentric repetition.

## Step 3 — Find Blue first

Increase leverage until the eccentric is challenging but unquestionably controllable.

A new trainee should be able to say:

> “I am fighting the descent, but I still own the movement.”

## Step 4 — Approach Orange gradually

Use tiny leverage adjustments.

For rings this may be only:
- 2–4 inches of foot movement;
- a slightly lower ring height;
- slightly less leg assistance.

The goal is not to jump from easy to extreme.

## Step 5 — Fight, do not “perform a slow negative”

The main internal cue is:

> **Try to stop. Let the load create the slow eccentric.**

Do not intentionally move slowly if you could hold the position. The method is defined by resisting a load that is overcoming you.

## Step 6 — End the rep before technique fails

A good eccentric rep ends when:
- the intended range is complete;
- the movement path begins to deform;
- control is deteriorating;
- you need to use momentum or a bailout.

## Step 7 — Reset completely

Each rep is a high-quality exposure, not a continuous burn set.

Reset the leverage, breathe, re-establish position, then begin the next rep.

---

# 4. Universal safety rules

Because leverage-based eccentric training can become extremely demanding without obvious external weight, the safety system is part of the exercise itself.

## Every exercise must have an escape path

The trainee should be able to answer before every rep:

> **If the load wins suddenly, where do I go?**

Examples:
- ring press: step forward;
- ring row: bend knees and step toward anchor;
- sissy squat: take more hand support;
- Nordic: catch with hands onto a padded surface;
- rollout: shorten the lever or drop to knees;
- pull-up: place foot onto the chair/box.

## Stop immediately for

- sharp pain;
- joint pinching;
- numbness or tingling;
- sudden tendon pain;
- uncontrolled acceleration;
- loss of shoulder, knee, hip, or trunk position;
- inability to use the planned escape.

## Beginner rule

A beginner does **not** need to chase maximal eccentric overload immediately.

The order is:

**movement path → escape → Blue control → Orange exposure**

---

# 5. Equipment standard

The Core 6 can be built with very little equipment.

## Primary equipment

- adjustable gymnastics rings or suspension straps;
- secure overhead anchor or pull-up bar;
- sturdy chair or box;
- padded floor/mat.

## Useful optional equipment

- sliders/furniture sliders;
- resistance band for Nordic assistance;
- towel or knee pad;
- wall/post for lower-body support.

## Anchor rule

Any ring or strap anchor must be capable of safely supporting substantially more than bodyweight and must not depend on a questionable door hinge, light trim, or unstable furniture.

# Core Exercise 1 — Ring Chest Press Eccentric

## Movement family

Horizontal push

## Primary targets

- pectorals;
- anterior deltoids;
- triceps;
- trunk stabilization.

## Why it is in the Core 6

This is probably the cleanest home example of leverage-based eccentric overload. The rings allow resistance to be adjusted in very small increments simply by moving the feet. The trainee can also escape instantly by stepping forward.

## The defining action

Start with the arms extended and the body organized. Then resist the body falling forward as the elbows bend and the rings move toward the sides of the chest.

The cue is:

> **Push as if you are trying to stay at the top. Let gravity slowly win.**

---

## Beginner setup

1. Set the rings around chest height.
2. Stand facing away from the anchor with one ring in each hand.
3. Keep the feet flat and begin almost upright.
4. Extend the arms in front of the chest.
5. Brace the trunk so the ribs do not flare and the hips do not sag.
6. Let the body lean forward as one unit.
7. Bend the elbows gradually while trying to press the rings away from you.
8. Step forward before you lose control.
9. Use the easier foot position to return to the top.

### Beginner calibration

Start so upright that you could easily perform the concentric press.

Then move the feet backward **a few inches at a time** until the eccentric becomes demanding.

The first training target is Blue, not Orange.

---

## Visual checkpoints

- head, rib cage, pelvis, and legs remain approximately one line;
- hands stay around chest level rather than drifting toward the face;
- elbows track in a comfortable diagonal path rather than flaring violently;
- shoulders do not dump forward at the bottom;
- feet remain planted until the planned exit.

## Pressure checkpoint

Pressure should be transmitted through:
- the hands into the rings;
- the feet into the floor;
- the chest/shoulders/triceps as the body moves forward.

## Tension checkpoint

You should primarily feel:
- chest;
- triceps;
- front shoulders;
- abdominal/trunk brace.

You should not primarily feel:
- front-of-shoulder pinching;
- wrist strain;
- low-back compression from rib flare.

## Intent checkpoint

Even though the body is descending, your intention is to **press upward/outward and stop the descent**.

---

## Leverage ladder

### Beginner exploration zone

1. **Near-upright ring press** — feet close to the anchor line.
2. **Shallow forward lean** — body angle modestly increased.
3. **Moderate forward lean** — more bodyweight committed to the rings.

Goal: learn shoulder path, trunk position, and the step-forward escape.

### Intermediate exploration zone

4. **Deep ring press** — greater lean and larger elbow bend.
5. **Near-horizontal ring push-up** — feet much farther from the anchor.
6. **Feet slightly elevated** — greater percentage of bodyweight transferred to the upper body.

### Advanced exploration zone

7. **Deep feet-elevated ring push-up eccentric**.
8. **Staggered-hand or unilateral-bias eccentric**.
9. **Assisted one-arm ring press eccentric**.
10. **Highly destabilized or long-lever ring press variants** only after the basic pattern is exceptionally strong.

The advanced goal is not to reach a specific stunt. It is to keep finding leverage where the eccentric is safely above concentric capacity.

---

## How to perform one training rep

1. Use an easy stance to reach locked-out arms.
2. Move the feet to the selected training position.
3. Brace.
4. Begin trying to press hard before the body starts moving.
5. Let the elbows bend only because the load overcomes you.
6. Continue resisting through the intended range.
7. Step forward to unload the rings.
8. Reset completely.

## Escape strategy

**Step forward immediately.**

The feet must remain positioned so one quick step significantly reduces loading.

## Common errors

- deliberately performing a theatrical slow negative instead of trying to stop;
- feet too far back, causing immediate collapse;
- elbows flaring aggressively;
- shoulders rolling forward at the bottom;
- ribs flaring / low back arching;
- trying to press concentrically back to the top from a supramaximal position.

## Regression hierarchy

1. step closer to the anchor;
2. raise the rings;
3. reduce range of motion;
4. use a staggered stance with more rear-foot assistance;
5. remain in Blue rather than Orange.

## Progression rule

Progress when:
- the current angle becomes stoppable;
- you can reverse the rep concentrically;
- the eccentric is clearly too easy while technique remains perfect.

Then change **one variable**:
- move feet slightly farther back;
- lower ring height slightly;
- increase range;
- reduce assistance.

# Core Exercise 2 — Ring Row Eccentric

## Movement family

Horizontal pull

## Primary targets

- lats;
- rhomboids;
- mid/lower trapezius;
- rear delts;
- biceps;
- trunk.

## Why it is in the Core 6

The row balances the ring press and is extremely scalable. It also allows the feet to remain on the ground, which makes the escape and leverage adjustment intuitive.

## The defining action

Begin with the chest close to the rings and resist being pulled away from them.

The cue is:

> **Keep pulling your chest toward the rings while gravity forces you toward straight arms.**

---

## Beginner setup

1. Face the ring anchor.
2. Hold the rings with arms bent and rings near the lower ribs/chest.
3. Keep the feet fairly close to the anchor so the torso is relatively upright.
4. Make the body one straight line from shoulders to ankles.
5. Set the shoulders away from the ears.
6. Start pulling before the elbows begin to open.
7. Resist the body lowering backward.
8. When the arms approach the planned end range, step toward the anchor.
9. Use the legs to return to the start.

## Visual checkpoints

- trunk stays rigid rather than folding at the hips;
- shoulders remain organized rather than shrugging;
- elbows travel backward naturally;
- neck stays neutral;
- chest remains the target between the rings.

## Pressure checkpoint

- heels/feet stay firmly grounded;
- hands maintain secure ring pressure;
- the feet provide enough friction that the body does not slide.

## Tension checkpoint

Primary:
- lats;
- mid-back;
- biceps;
- rear shoulders.

Avoid:
- neck strain;
- elbow pain;
- low-back extension.

## Intent checkpoint

Try to **continue pulling the rings toward your rib cage** even while the elbows are opening.

---

## Leverage ladder

### Beginner exploration zone

1. **Very upright row**.
2. **Moderate lean**.
3. **Deeper lean with straight body line**.

### Intermediate exploration zone

4. **Near-horizontal body row**.
5. **Feet elevated slightly**.
6. **Feet elevated higher / deeper eccentric range**.

### Advanced exploration zone

7. **Archer-row eccentric** — more load shifted to one arm.
8. **Assisted one-arm row eccentric**.
9. **One-arm dominant eccentric with minimal second-arm assistance**.

The advanced versions must preserve shoulder control. One-arm loading is never worth losing scapular position.

---

## Calibration method

Start at a body angle where a concentric row is easy.

Walk the feet forward in small increments.

At each position ask:

- Can I hold the top?
- Can I lower under complete control?
- Could I pull myself back up from halfway?

Blue: you probably can still stop.

Orange: you can no longer return concentrically, but you can keep the eccentric slow and clean.

## Training rep

1. Stand slightly more upright and pull to the top.
2. Walk feet into the chosen leverage.
3. Set shoulders and trunk.
4. Pull maximally toward the rings.
5. Allow gravity to open the elbows only as it overcomes the pull.
6. Continue until the planned end range.
7. Bend knees / step toward anchor.
8. Reset.

## Escape strategy

- bend the knees;
- step toward the anchor;
- become upright immediately.

## Common errors

- hips sagging;
- shrugging;
- chin reaching toward the rings;
- rings drifting far apart;
- deliberately relaxing at the bottom;
- progressing foot position too aggressively.

## Regression hierarchy

1. stand more upright;
2. shorten range;
3. keep one foot farther back for assistance;
4. perform controlled Blue eccentrics;
5. reduce total reps.

## Progression rule

Move the feet farther forward only when the current angle is consistently controllable and begins to permit a concentric reversal.

---

# Core Exercise 3 — Supported Sissy-Squat Eccentric

## Movement family

Knee-dominant lower body / quadriceps

## Primary targets

- quadriceps;
- anterior chain;
- knee-extensor control.

## Why it is in the Core 6

This exercise provides a strong quad-focused eccentric stimulus without requiring barbells. Hand support allows the trainee to precisely regulate how much bodyweight reaches the legs and creates a reliable emergency exit.

## The defining action

Keep the body long while the knees travel forward and the body descends backward/downward.

The cue is:

> **Try to straighten the knees and stay tall while the load forces you deeper.**

---

## Beginner setup

1. Stand beside a sturdy post, rings, rail, or doorframe-safe support.
2. Take light support with one or both hands.
3. Rise slightly onto the forefoot.
4. Keep the hips relatively extended instead of sitting backward like a normal squat.
5. Allow the knees to travel forward.
6. Keep the torso and thighs forming a relatively long line.
7. Actively try to extend the knees while the body lowers.
8. Increase hand assistance immediately if control deteriorates.
9. Use the arms to help return to standing.

## Visual checkpoints

- hips do not shoot backward;
- knees travel forward in line with the feet;
- trunk does not fold sharply at the waist;
- heels may rise, but forefoot pressure remains stable;
- support is close enough to use instantly.

## Pressure checkpoint

- forefoot remains loaded;
- hands provide adjustable assistance rather than passive hanging;
- knees track over the toes.

## Tension checkpoint

Primary:
- quadriceps;
- especially strong tension above the knees as depth increases.

Avoid:
- sharp kneecap pain;
- uncontrolled ankle collapse;
- low-back discomfort from excessive arching.

## Intent checkpoint

Try to **straighten the knees without actually rising**. The descent occurs because bodyweight exceeds your current knee-extension capacity at that leverage.

---

## Leverage ladder

### Beginner exploration zone

1. **Two-hand supported shallow sissy eccentric**.
2. **Two-hand supported deeper eccentric**.
3. **One-hand supported eccentric with substantial assistance**.

### Intermediate exploration zone

4. **One-hand light-support eccentric**.
5. **Minimal fingertip support**.
6. **Longer/deeper eccentric while maintaining body line**.

### Advanced exploration zone

7. **Unassisted sissy-squat eccentric**.
8. **Greater range / elevated heel setup** where appropriate.
9. **Loaded or single-leg-biased sissy eccentric** only after extensive mastery.

The advanced spectrum should be treated cautiously. More knee flexion is not automatically better.

---

## Calibration method

Unlike ring upper-body work, the primary “weight selector” is **how much support your arms provide**.

Start with both hands providing obvious assistance.

Then progressively reduce support:

- firm two-hand support;
- lighter two-hand support;
- one hand;
- fingertips;
- minimal/no support.

Orange occurs when the legs cannot stop the descent on their own, but the movement remains smooth and the hands can instantly rescue the position.

## Training rep

1. Stand tall with support ready.
2. Rise onto the forefoot.
3. Begin trying to extend the knees.
4. Reduce arm assistance until the legs begin yielding slowly.
5. Fight the descent while maintaining the long body line.
6. Increase hand assistance before form breaks.
7. Use the support to return to the top.

## Escape strategy

**Pull with the arms and stand up.**

The hands should never be too far away to rescue the rep.

## Common errors

- sitting hips backward into a conventional squat;
- dropping too quickly;
- chasing extreme depth;
- knees collapsing inward;
- using so little support that the bottom becomes uncontrolled;
- mistaking joint pain for useful quad tension.

## Regression hierarchy

1. increase hand support;
2. reduce depth;
3. use both hands;
4. keep the heels slightly lower if forefoot balance is difficult;
5. remain in Blue.

## Progression rule

Reduce hand assistance before chasing deeper range.

Progression sequence:

**control → less support → more range → harder leverage**

---

# Core Exercise 4 — Assisted Nordic Hamstring Eccentric

## Movement family

Knee-flexion posterior chain

## Primary targets

- hamstrings;
- especially knee-flexor function;
- glutes/trunk as stabilizers.

## Why it is in the Core 6

Nordic eccentrics produce an unusually strong hamstring stimulus using bodyweight alone. The challenge is that a full Nordic is too difficult for most beginners, so assistance is not optional — it is the resistance dial.

## Required setup

The ankles must be anchored securely enough that the setup cannot slide or tip.

Acceptable options may include:
- purpose-built Nordic anchor;
- heavy, stable furniture only if verified safe;
- a partner;
- secure strap anchor.

Use a thick pad under the knees.

Rings or a resistance band can provide upper-body assistance.

## The defining action

From tall kneeling, keep the hips extended while resisting the torso falling forward.

Cue:

> **Pull your heels toward your butt and try to stay tall while gravity opens the knees.**

---

## Beginner setup

1. Kneel on a padded surface.
2. Secure the ankles.
3. Keep hips extended rather than sitting back.
4. Hold rings or assistance in front of you.
5. Squeeze glutes lightly and brace trunk.
6. Begin leaning forward from the knees as one long line.
7. Pull the heels backward/down into the anchor as if trying to bend the knees.
8. Let the assistance regulate speed.
9. Catch the bottom with the hands if needed.
10. Use the hands/rings to return to tall kneeling.

## Visual checkpoints

- hip angle remains relatively open;
- torso and thighs travel as one line;
- no sudden hinge at the hips;
- ankles remain secure;
- hands are ready to catch.

## Pressure checkpoint

- knees remain comfortably padded;
- ankles/heels press safely into the anchor;
- assistance is tensioned before the rep begins.

## Tension checkpoint

Primary:
- hamstrings, often strongly near the back of the thigh.

Avoid:
- knee pain;
- cramping severe enough to change mechanics;
- calf/ankle pressure from a poor anchor;
- low-back extension.

## Intent checkpoint

Try to **flex the knees and pull yourself back upright** throughout the descent.

If you are simply falling slowly without that intent, the rep is not being performed correctly.

---

## Leverage ladder

### Beginner exploration zone

1. **Heavy ring assistance, short range**.
2. **Heavy ring assistance, larger range**.
3. **Moderate assistance through full comfortable range**.

### Intermediate exploration zone

4. **Light ring/band assistance**.
5. **Unassisted upper range + assisted lower range**.
6. **Long-range unassisted eccentric with hand catch**.

### Advanced exploration zone

7. **Full unassisted Nordic eccentric**.
8. **Slower/stronger full-range eccentric where the athlete can nearly stop**.
9. **Loaded Nordic eccentric or extended-lever variation** only after full unassisted competence.

The system does not require everyone to reach weighted Nordics. For many people, the full unassisted Nordic remains an advanced endpoint.

---

## Calibration method

Assistance is the resistance dial.

Use enough ring/band assistance that the descent is smooth.

Then reduce assistance gradually.

Blue: you can control the entire rep and probably stop temporarily.

Orange: you are pulling maximally with the hamstrings yet still lengthening, while the rings/band merely prevent collapse.

Red: you fall forward faster than you can organize or must catch abruptly.

## Training rep

1. Establish tall kneeling.
2. Pre-tension the assistance.
3. Pull heels into the anchor.
4. Reduce upper-body assistance until the body starts descending.
5. Fight the descent continuously.
6. Increase assistance or catch before losing body line.
7. Reset with arms/assistance.

## Escape strategy

- hands in front ready to catch;
- padded surface;
- assistance positioned so you can pull yourself back immediately.

## Common errors

- hinging at the hips;
- losing glute/trunk position;
- using an unsafe ankle anchor;
- removing too much assistance too early;
- trying to return concentrically using the hamstrings when the eccentric is supramaximal;
- high volume before hamstrings have adapted.

## Regression hierarchy

1. increase ring/band assistance;
2. shorten range;
3. catch earlier;
4. reduce eccentric intensity;
5. perform fewer reps.

## Progression rule

Reduce assistance before increasing range only if the current range remains perfectly organized.

For many beginners, **range and assistance may need to progress independently**.

# Core Exercise 5 — Ring Fallout / Ab Rollout Eccentric

## Movement family

Anterior core / anti-extension

## Primary targets

- rectus abdominis;
- obliques;
- serratus;
- lats and shoulder stabilizers;
- trunk stiffness.

## Why it is in the Core 6

The fallout provides a scalable trunk eccentric using the same ring system as the upper-body exercises. The body angle can range from almost vertical to extremely demanding.

## The defining action

Maintain rib-to-pelvis control while the arms travel forward and the body lengthens.

Cue:

> **Pull the ribs toward the pelvis and try to stop the body from opening.**

---

## Beginner setup

1. Set the rings around chest/shoulder height.
2. Stand facing away from or beneath the anchor depending on setup.
3. Hold the rings with straight or softly bent elbows.
4. Begin nearly upright.
5. Slightly tuck the ribs/pelvis into a strong trunk position.
6. Allow the arms to move forward as the body leans.
7. Resist extension through the abdomen.
8. Stop before the low back arches or shoulders lose position.
9. Step forward / pull rings back to reset.

## Visual checkpoints

- ribs remain controlled;
- pelvis does not dump forward;
- low back does not visibly sag;
- arms travel symmetrically;
- head remains neutral.

## Pressure checkpoint

- feet stay firmly planted;
- ring pressure remains even;
- shoulders stay active rather than hanging passively.

## Tension checkpoint

Primary:
- abdomen;
- deep trunk;
- serratus/lats as the arms move overhead.

Avoid:
- low-back compression;
- shoulder pinching;
- hip-flexor cramping dominating the exercise.

## Intent checkpoint

Try to **pull the arms back and close the rib-to-pelvis distance** even while leverage forces the body to lengthen.

---

## Leverage ladder

### Beginner exploration zone

1. **Very upright ring fallout**.
2. **Moderate standing lean**.
3. **Greater standing lean with limited range**.

### Intermediate exploration zone

4. **Deep standing ring fallout**.
5. **Kneeling ring rollout / slider rollout**.
6. **Long-range kneeling rollout**.

### Advanced exploration zone

7. **Near-horizontal standing fallout**.
8. **Standing rollout with long lever**.
9. **Feet-elevated or highly extended ring rollout variants** where appropriate.

Advanced progressions must not be earned by simply allowing lumbar extension.

---

## Calibration method

The resistance increases rapidly as the body becomes more horizontal and the arms travel farther forward.

Change only small amounts:
- move feet;
- lower rings;
- increase arm reach slightly.

Blue: hard abdominal control but stoppable.

Orange: you cannot return from the deepest point without stepping/resetting, but you can resist the opening cleanly.

## Training rep

1. Start in the easy reset position.
2. Move feet to training leverage.
3. Brace before the arms move.
4. Try to pull the rings back toward you.
5. Allow the body to lengthen only as the leverage overcomes the brace.
6. Stop at the final clean position.
7. Step forward or reduce leverage.
8. Reset without performing a forced concentric.

## Escape strategy

- step forward;
- shorten arm reach;
- bend knees slightly if necessary;
- lower to knees if using a kneeling/standing hybrid setup.

## Common errors

- low-back sag;
- chasing range rather than trunk control;
- shoulders collapsing toward ears;
- arms moving farther than the trunk can control;
- using momentum;
- progressing to standing rollouts too early.

## Regression hierarchy

1. stand more upright;
2. shorten arm travel;
3. raise rings;
4. bend knees slightly;
5. move to kneeling rollout;
6. remain in Blue.

## Progression rule

Progress the body angle only when the current position remains rigid enough that the abs — not the low back — are clearly limiting the rep.

# Core Exercise 6 — Assisted Pull-Up Eccentric

## Movement family

Vertical pull

## Primary targets

- lats;
- biceps;
- lower/mid traps;
- scapular stabilizers;
- grip.

## Why it is in the Core 6

The pull-up eccentric provides a vertical pulling pattern that the ring row cannot fully replace. A chair or box makes the top position accessible to nearly anyone and allows the return phase to remain assisted.

## Required setup

Use:
- a securely mounted pull-up bar or rings rated for bodyweight;
- a sturdy chair/box positioned so one or both feet can reach it throughout the rep.

The box is not optional during early learning. It is the safety and load-adjustment system.

## The defining action

Begin near the top and resist the elbows/shoulders opening as the body lowers.

Cue:

> **Keep pulling the bar/rings toward your chest while your body is forced downward.**

---

## Beginner setup

1. Place a chair/box directly below or slightly in front of the bar.
2. Step up until the elbows are comfortably bent and the shoulders are active.
3. Keep one or both feet on the box initially.
4. Organize the ribs and trunk.
5. Begin pulling before reducing foot pressure.
6. Gradually unload the feet until the arms take most of the bodyweight.
7. Resist the body lowering.
8. Reapply foot pressure at any time to control speed.
9. Finish with the shoulders still active rather than dropping into a passive dead hang.
10. Step back to the top for the next rep.

## Visual checkpoints

- shoulders remain away from the ears as long as possible;
- trunk remains controlled;
- elbows open smoothly;
- legs do not swing;
- head does not aggressively crane over the bar.

## Pressure checkpoint

- grip secure;
- foot remains close enough to box to rescue the rep;
- assistance is adjusted through the foot, not by kicking.

## Tension checkpoint

Primary:
- lats;
- biceps;
- upper/mid back;
- grip.

Avoid:
- shoulder joint hanging;
- elbow pain;
- neck tension dominating.

## Intent checkpoint

Try to **continue pulling yourself upward** throughout the entire downward movement.

---

## Leverage / assistance ladder

### Beginner exploration zone

1. **Two-foot assisted eccentric** — substantial foot pressure.
2. **Two-foot light assistance**.
3. **One-foot assisted eccentric**.

### Intermediate exploration zone

4. **Minimal toe assistance**.
5. **Jump/step to top + unassisted partial eccentric**.
6. **Full-range unassisted eccentric**.

### Advanced exploration zone

7. **Longer full-range eccentric with stronger top control**.
8. **Chest-to-ring / deeper range eccentric** where shoulders tolerate it.
9. **Weighted eccentric**.
10. **Archer or one-arm-biased eccentric** only after extensive bilateral strength.

A full unassisted eccentric is already a substantial achievement. There is no need to race toward one-arm work.

---

## Calibration method

The easiest resistance dial is foot pressure.

Begin with enough pressure that the rep is unquestionably controlled.

Reduce assistance gradually during the rep.

Blue: the arms carry most bodyweight, but you can still stop or add foot pressure effortlessly.

Orange: with little/no foot assistance, you are pulling maximally but still lowering slowly; the foot remains close enough to rescue instantly.

Red: shoulders drop, descent accelerates, or the box cannot be reached quickly enough.

## Training rep

1. Step to top.
2. Organize shoulders and ribs.
3. Pull hard.
4. Reduce foot assistance.
5. Fight the descent.
6. Reapply foot support before shoulder control is lost.
7. Step back to top.
8. Reset fully.

## Escape strategy

**Put the foot back on the box.**

The box height and placement should make this possible throughout the working range.

## Common errors

- jumping into an uncontrolled negative;
- relaxing into the bottom;
- excessive neck craning;
- swinging;
- box too low or too far away;
- grip failure before back muscles are trained;
- doing too many hard negatives.

## Regression hierarchy

1. increase foot assistance;
2. use both feet;
3. shorten range;
4. stop before the bottom hang;
5. use neutral-grip rings if more comfortable;
6. perform ring rows until vertical pulling control improves.

## Progression rule

Progress by reducing assistance before adding external load.

A clean full-bodyweight eccentric should be mastered before weighted negatives are considered.

# 6. The Core 6 progression map at a glance

| Pattern | Beginner | Intermediate | Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ring Chest Press | upright → modest lean | deep lean → near-horizontal → feet elevated | unilateral bias / assisted one-arm |
| Ring Row | upright → moderate lean | near-horizontal → feet elevated | archer / one-arm-biased |
| Sissy Squat | heavy two-hand support | light one-hand support → fingertip | unassisted → loaded/biased |
| Nordic | heavy assistance + short range | lighter assistance → partial unassisted | full Nordic → loaded |
| Ring Fallout | upright short range | deep standing / kneeling long range | standing long-lever / advanced fallout |
| Pull-Up | two-foot assistance | one-foot/toe assist → full eccentric | weighted / unilateral-biased |

The table is not a calendar. It is a map.

A trainee may simultaneously be:
- intermediate at rows;
- beginner at Nordics;
- advanced at chest press;
- beginner at pull-up.

That is normal.

# 7. How the trainee finds the correct starting point

For each exercise:

1. Choose a clearly easy Beginner-zone position.
2. Perform one controlled eccentric.
3. Increase leverage slightly.
4. Repeat only until the rep becomes meaningfully difficult.
5. Stop the calibration process before technique deteriorates.
6. Use the easiest position that gives the desired training zone.

## First-session rule

The first exposure should end in **Blue**, even if Orange is available.

This is especially important for:
- Nordics;
- pull-up negatives;
- sissy squats.

The athlete should experience what the movement feels like before deliberately creating a true supramaximal condition.

---

# 8. How progression works

Progression is not “add five pounds.”

Instead, change one mechanical variable at a time.

## Ring exercises

Possible progression variables:
- foot distance from anchor;
- ring height;
- foot elevation;
- range of motion;
- amount of leg assistance;
- bilateral → asymmetrical → unilateral bias.

## Nordic

Possible progression variables:
- amount of ring/band assistance;
- unassisted portion of range;
- total range;
- external loading only after full competence.

## Sissy squat

Possible progression variables:
- hand assistance;
- range;
- body angle;
- external load only later.

## Pull-up

Possible progression variables:
- amount of foot assistance;
- range;
- duration of clean eccentric;
- bodyweight → external load;
- bilateral → unilateral bias only very late.

# 9. Learning-mode completion criteria

A trainee has learned an exercise when they can:

1. explain the movement path;
2. demonstrate the safe reset;
3. demonstrate the emergency escape;
4. identify the primary muscles expected to work;
5. distinguish Blue from Orange and Red;
6. find a repeatable training leverage without guessing wildly;
7. perform at least two clean controlled eccentric reps at the chosen leverage;
8. stop the set before technique failure;
9. adjust the leverage easier or harder by one small step;
10. return the exercise to an easier setting when recovery or soreness requires it.

Only after these criteria are met should the exercise be programmed as true supramaximal eccentric work.

# 10. Core coaching language

To keep Workout Mode simple, each exercise eventually reduces to three or four high-value cues.

## Ring Chest Press

**Brace → Press hard → Fight forward → Step out**

## Ring Row

**One body line → Pull chest in → Fight away → Step in**

## Sissy Squat

**Stay long → Knees forward → Extend hard → Use hands**

## Nordic

**Hips long → Pull heels back → Fight forward → Catch**

## Ring Fallout

**Ribs down → Pull rings back → Fight the opening → Step in**

## Pull-Up

**Shoulders active → Pull up → Fight down → Foot to box**

# Working principle to preserve

> **Every exercise is a spectrum. Every spectrum is a progression. Every workout teaches the trainee to find the correct point on that progression for today.**

The goal is not to chase the hardest-looking variation. The goal is to create a resistance that is **too hard to lift, slow enough to fight, clean enough to control, and easy enough to escape safely.**
