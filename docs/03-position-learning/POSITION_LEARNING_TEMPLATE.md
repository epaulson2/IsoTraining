# Position Learning Template

## Goal

Teach the trainee to **construct, feel, and intentionally control the position before training it under fatigue**. Learn Mode is intentionally more detailed than Workout Mode.

The learning sequence must teach three different things separately before combining them:

1. **Geometry** — where the body goes.
2. **Tension** — where muscular effort should be felt.
3. **Intent** — what the trainee is actively trying to do even when little visible movement occurs.

For Extreme Isometrics, this distinction is essential. Two people can look nearly identical while one is passively surviving the position and the other is performing the intended exercise.

## Learning Sequence

### Step 1 — Establish the base
Set hand / foot placement, stance width, grip, external supports, strap routing or contact points without worrying about maximal depth.

### Step 2 — Build the gross geometry
Set the major visible landmarks: knees / elbows, hips / shoulders, shin / forearm orientation, torso angle and head position.

### Step 3 — Organize pelvis, rib cage and spine
Find the intended relationship between pelvis, trunk and rib cage. Avoid using unnecessary spinal motion to manufacture additional range.

### Step 4 — Establish pressure distribution
Learn exactly where force should be transmitted through the feet, hands, support surface, bar or strap. If the exercise has a distinctive pressure cue — heel, forefoot, tripod foot, palm, etc. — specify it explicitly.

### Step 5 — Find the working depth / joint angle
Move into the deepest or longest position that preserves the required geometry and active muscular control. Depth is earned; it is not the primary objective.

### Step 6 — Learn the primary muscular action
At low effort, teach the principal contraction in isolation. Describe it as an action rather than merely naming a muscle — e.g. “drag the front foot backward without letting it move.”

### Step 7 — Learn the opposing / stabilizing action
Add the relevant co-contraction, antagonist contribution, trunk stabilization or second-limb action.

### Step 8 — Combine the force vectors
Create the full internal force relationship while keeping the visible position stable. For complex exercises, use simple arrows or directional language.

### Step 9 — Add Extreme-Iso intent
Attempt to move farther into the prescribed extreme through muscular effort while preserving the established geometry. The trainee should not obtain extra depth by relaxing into passive tissues or abandoning alignment.

### Step 10 — Short competency hold
Perform a brief technically clean hold. The purpose is skill verification, not fatigue.

### Step 11 — Practice the exit
Return from the position deliberately. Long-duration or high-intent work can make an otherwise easy exit unexpectedly difficult.

## Four Checkpoints

### 1. Visual checkpoint — Geometry
**Does the body look like the reference position?**

Use observable landmarks. Avoid vague cues such as “good posture.” Specify what can actually be checked.

Examples:
- shin approximately vertical;
- forearm approximately vertical;
- heel remains down;
- pelvis remains square;
- trunk remains upright.

### 2. Pressure checkpoint — Contact forces
**Am I loading the intended parts of the floor / support / strap?**

Pressure distribution can materially change the exercise even when the visible pose looks similar.

### 3. Tension checkpoint — Muscular sensation
**Is muscular effort concentrated where intended?**

Differentiate useful muscular tension from sharp joint pressure, pinching, numbness, tingling, or passive tissue strain.

### 4. Intent checkpoint — Internal action
**Am I actively attempting the correct action?**

Describe the action in plain language. “Contract the hamstrings” is inferior to “try to drag your heel backward without allowing the foot to move.”

## Error Hierarchy

When technique deteriorates, correct errors in this order:

1. **Pain / neurological symptoms** — stop or regress.
2. **Loss of required geometry** — reduce depth, leverage, duration or intensity.
3. **Loss of active intent** — reset rather than merely surviving the clock.
4. **Wrong pressure distribution** — reposition the base or reduce load.
5. **Minor cosmetic differences** — tolerate individual variation if the essential mechanics remain intact.

## Regression Hierarchy

Prefer regressions that preserve the exercise's defining mechanics:

1. external support for balance;
2. reduced bodyweight / leverage;
3. shallower working angle;
4. lower contraction intent;
5. shorter exposure;
6. simplified unilateral-to-bilateral version when necessary.

Do not automatically regress by changing the defining joint geometry if another option can preserve it.

## Why This Detail Matters

Every major positional cue should include a short explanation of why it matters biomechanically or functionally. Avoid presenting arbitrary rules without rationale.

Where the historical Schroeder / EVO cue is known but the physiological explanation is speculative, preserve the cue while clearly labeling the explanation as historical rather than established science.

## Provenance Standard

Every exercise instruction should distinguish among:

- **Schroeder / EVO Original** — directly described by Schroeder or strong historical EVO documentation.
- **Schroeder-Derived** — close teaching variation or regression based on the original position.
- **Evidence-Based Extension** — developed for this project using the Extreme-Iso principles plus conventional biomechanics / exercise science.

Do not silently upgrade a derivative cue into an original Schroeder prescription.

## Completion Standard

A trainee has learned the position when they can:

1. enter it without coaching;
2. reproduce the key visual landmarks;
3. establish the intended pressure distribution;
4. identify the primary muscular tension locations;
5. create the correct internal force intent;
6. maintain all critical cues during a short hold;
7. recognize the major failure modes;
8. exit safely and deliberately.

Only then should normal Workout Mode prescriptions replace the full tutorial.

## Workout Mode Conversion

After competency, reduce the tutorial to:

- exercise name;
- prescribed duration / intensity;
- maximum three critical cues;
- optional regression / variation note.

The Workout Mode cues should correspond directly to the highest-value checkpoints from Learn Mode rather than introducing new technique language.
