# Position Learning Template

## Goal

Teach the trainee to **construct and understand the position before training it under fatigue**. Learn Mode is intentionally more detailed than Workout Mode.

## Learning Sequence

### Step 1 — Establish the base
Set hand / foot placement and external supports without worrying about maximal depth.

### Step 2 — Align the major joints
Set knees / elbows, hips / shoulders and trunk orientation.

### Step 3 — Organize pelvis, rib cage and spine
Find the intended stacked / braced relationship without unnecessary rigidity.

### Step 4 — Establish pressure distribution
Learn where force should go through the feet, hands, support surface or strap.

### Step 5 — Learn the primary muscular action
Practice the main contraction at low effort without adding the full Extreme-Iso demand.

### Step 6 — Learn the opposing / stabilizing action
Add the relevant co-contraction or stabilization strategy.

### Step 7 — Combine the forces
Create the internal force relationship while keeping the visible position stable.

### Step 8 — Add Extreme-Iso intent
Attempt to pull / push farther into the intended position while preserving geometry.

### Step 9 — Short competency hold
Perform a brief technically clean hold. The goal is not fatigue; it is demonstrating control.

## Three Checkpoints

### Visual checkpoint
**Does the body look like the reference position?**

Use observable landmarks and, eventually, diagrams / photos.

### Tension checkpoint
**Is the effort being felt primarily where intended?**

Differentiate muscular effort from passive joint pressure or pinching.

### Intent checkpoint
**Am I actively attempting the correct internal action?**

This is essential because two trainees can look identical while one is passively hanging and the other is creating the intended Extreme-Iso contraction.

## Why This Detail Matters

Every major positional cue should eventually include a short explanation of why it matters biomechanically or functionally. Avoid presenting arbitrary rules without rationale.

## Completion Standard

A trainee has learned the position when they can:

1. enter it without coaching;
2. identify the primary pressure / tension locations;
3. create the correct internal force intent;
4. maintain the key alignment cues during a short hold;
5. exit safely and deliberately.

Only then should normal Workout Mode prescriptions replace the full tutorial.

## Workout Mode Conversion

After competency, reduce the tutorial to:

- exercise name;
- prescribed duration / intensity;
- maximum three critical cues;
- optional regression / variation note.
