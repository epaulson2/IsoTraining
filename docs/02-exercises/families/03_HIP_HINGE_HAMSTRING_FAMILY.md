# Hip Hinge / Hamstring Family

## Status

**Validated family draft — strong historical support for the Standing Glute-Ham / Standing Hamstring position; strap variants are modern extensions and remain clearly labeled.**

## Family role

This family trains posterior-chain positional control at long muscle lengths, with special emphasis on the hamstrings, glutes, hip hinge mechanics, and the ability to create active downward intent without collapsing through the lumbar spine.

## Primary versions

### A. Iso-Extreme Standing Glute-Ham / Standing Hamstring
- **Type:** Integrated
- **Provenance:** Schroeder / EVO core
- **Use:** Foundational posterior-chain Extreme Iso

### B. Supported Standing Glute-Ham
- **Type:** Integrated regression
- **Provenance:** Schroeder-derived teaching regression
- **Use:** Learn Mode, balance assistance, and controlled depth acquisition

### C. Strap-Assisted Targeted Hamstring Hinge
- **Type:** Targeted / hybrid
- **Provenance:** Evidence-based extension
- **Use:** Increase hamstring intent while reducing balance and whole-body stabilization as limiting factors

### D. Strap-Assisted Hamstring Curl Iso
- **Type:** Targeted
- **Provenance:** Evidence-based extension
- **Use:** Direct knee-flexor isometric loading at a chosen long-muscle-length position

# A. Iso-Extreme Standing Glute-Ham / Standing Hamstring

## Historical support

The Standing Glute-Ham / Standing Hamstring appears repeatedly in surviving Schroeder / EVO exercise lists and is one of the most consistently documented posterior-chain positions in the system. Historical descriptions emphasize active pulling into a deeper hip-hinge position rather than merely holding a passive stretch.

## Purpose

Train long-position hamstring and glute control while maintaining an organized hip hinge and actively creating force into a deeper position.

## Setup geometry

Surviving coaching descriptions commonly use:

- feet close together, often with heels touching or nearly touching;
- toes turned outward approximately 30–45 degrees in some versions;
- knees kept softly bent rather than locked;
- movement occurring primarily through the hip joint rather than lumbar flexion;
- upper back allowed to remain comparatively relaxed rather than rigidly extended;
- arms either hanging, crossed, or otherwise positioned so they do not become the limiting factor.

The exact toe angle is best treated as a historical variation rather than a universal requirement. The non-negotiable technical priority is a true hip hinge with active hamstring loading and no painful passive end-range dumping.

## Pressure distribution

- Maintain stable foot contact and avoid drifting excessively onto the forefoot.
- Historical user reports and later coaching variants sometimes bias pressure toward the heels by elevating the toes, but this should be considered an optional progression / teaching tool rather than a defining Schroeder requirement.
- The trainee should feel the posterior chain taking tension as the torso moves downward.

## Active intent

The defining feature is **active pulling deeper into the hinge**.

Use the following sequence:

1. create a small knee bend and stable foot pressure;
2. hinge at the hips while keeping the lumbar spine from becoming the primary source of motion;
3. actively pull the torso downward using the abdominals / hip-flexor complex;
4. simultaneously maintain active glute and hamstring tension;
5. continue attempting to move deeper while the posterior chain lengthens and resists the descent;
6. avoid turning the exercise into a passive hamstring stretch.

Historical descriptions also include the image of the glutes and hamstrings trying to pull the feet backward into the floor, similar to the force direction of a standing long jump, while the trunk continues to be drawn downward.

## What should remain stable

- feet remain planted and controlled;
- knees maintain a small, consistent bend unless using a later validated straightening progression;
- motion occurs through the hips more than through the lumbar spine;
- no sudden rounding or collapsing into the bottom position;
- breathing remains controlled rather than fully braced and held.

## What should you feel

Primary muscular tension should be distributed through:

- hamstrings;
- glutes;
- deep trunk / abdominal musculature;
- some hip-flexor activity as part of the downward-pulling intent.

Avoid:

- sharp pain behind the knee;
- nerve-like tension, tingling, or numbness;
- focal low-back pain;
- passive stretch sensation with little muscular work.

## Learn Mode sequence

### Step 1 — Establish the stance
Stand with feet close together or at a narrow, stable width. Use a wall, rack, or dowel for balance if needed.

### Step 2 — Unlock the knees
Keep a soft bend rather than locking the knees into hyperextension.

### Step 3 — Learn the hip hinge
Practice pushing the hips backward while the torso inclines forward. Keep the lumbar spine from becoming the main moving segment.

### Step 4 — Find posterior-chain tension
Hinge only until the hamstrings are clearly loaded without nerve-like symptoms or joint discomfort.

### Step 5 — Learn downward intent
At low effort, use the abs and hip flexors to actively draw the torso farther downward.

### Step 6 — Add posterior-chain opposition
Maintain active glute and hamstring tension while continuing to pull deeper.

### Step 7 — Add foot-drag intent
Without actually moving the feet, imagine dragging them backward into the floor. This increases glute / hamstring recruitment and helps prevent passive hanging.

### Step 8 — Combine the force vectors
Maintain the hinge while the anterior chain pulls the torso deeper and the posterior chain remains actively loaded.

### Step 9 — Add Extreme-Iso intent
Continue attempting to deepen the hinge slowly while preserving hip mechanics and muscular control.

### Step 10 — Competency hold
Demonstrate 10–15 seconds of controlled intent without lumbar collapse, knee locking, or passive stretching.

### Step 11 — Practice the exit
Reduce intent first, then reverse the hinge under control. Do not snap upright after a fatigued long-duration hold.

## Visual checkpoint

- hips are clearly flexed;
- torso is inclined from the hip joint;
- knees remain softly bent;
- spine is controlled rather than collapsing segmentally;
- feet remain stable.

## Tension checkpoint

The hamstrings and glutes should feel actively loaded, not merely stretched.

## Intent checkpoint

You should be **actively trying to pull deeper into the hinge** while simultaneously maintaining hamstring / glute tension.

## Common compensations

- **Lumbar rounding dominates the movement:** reduce depth and relearn the hip hinge.
- **Knees lock backward:** restore a small bend.
- **Passive stretch replaces muscular effort:** decrease depth and re-cue downward pull + posterior-chain tension.
- **Weight shifts to toes:** restore more balanced foot pressure or use a heel-biased regression if needed.
- **Head lifts aggressively:** keep the neck relaxed and organized with the torso.
- **Breath holding:** lower effort and restore controlled breathing.

## Regressions

- hands supported on rack / wall;
- shallower hinge;
- shorter holds;
- lower contraction intent;
- slightly wider stance for balance;
- partial-range hinge with a dowel along the spine for feedback.

## Advanced variations

- deeper controlled hinge;
- longer accumulated duration;
- greater voluntary downward-pulling intent;
- toe-elevated / heel-biased version as a later coaching variation;
- Extreme Slow hinge;
- later integration with elevated deadlift / force-absorption methods where appropriate.

## Workout-mode cues

**Soft knees • hinge at the hips • actively pull deeper while the hamstrings stay loaded**

# B. Supported Standing Glute-Ham

## Purpose

Teach the same geometry and internal force relationship while removing balance as the primary limitation.

## Setup

- Use a rack, wall, suspension support, or light fingertip support.
- The support should assist balance only, not hold the body up.
- Preserve the same soft-knee and hip-hinge mechanics as the integrated version.

## Learn Mode use

This is the preferred entry point for trainees who cannot yet distinguish hip flexion from lumbar flexion or who lose balance before meaningful hamstring tension develops.

## Workout-mode cues

**Use support only for balance • keep soft knees • pull deeper from the hips**

# C. Strap-Assisted Targeted Hamstring Hinge

## Concept

Use a non-elastic strap to create a fixed resistance vector that lets the trainee generate strong hamstring / glute intent while maintaining a chosen hip-flexion angle.

## Why it belongs

The integrated Standing Glute-Ham is valuable because it requires whole-body organization. The strap version serves a different purpose:

- more precise joint-angle selection;
- reduced balance demand;
- easier intensity scaling;
- clearer local hamstring / glute effort;
- a bridge between Extreme-Iso positional work and more conventional overcoming isometrics.

## Preferred setup options

### Option 1 — Strap under feet, handles in hands
- Stand on the center of a structurally rated strap.
- Adjust the strap so tension becomes maximal at the intended hinge depth.
- Hinge to the selected angle and attempt to extend the hips / stand taller while the strap prevents movement.

This is an **overcoming isometric**, not a classic Schroeder Iso-Extreme. It should therefore be labeled as a targeted companion exercise rather than a substitute for the integrated position.

### Option 2 — Strap anchored forward
- Anchor the strap securely in front of the body.
- Hold the handles while hinging backward into a long hamstring position.
- Attempt to pull / extend against the fixed endpoint while maintaining the chosen hip angle.

Exact anchor geometry should be chosen conservatively to avoid turning the exercise into a low-back-dominant pull.

## Learn Mode sequence

1. Establish the hinge without the strap.
2. Set a moderate hip-flexion angle.
3. Adjust strap length so there is firm resistance at that exact angle.
4. Brace lightly and confirm the hamstrings / glutes are the primary tissues producing force.
5. Build force gradually rather than jumping directly to maximal intent.
6. Keep the torso and hip angle unchanged during the contraction.
7. End the set if the lumbar spine begins to move more than the hips.

## Workout-mode cues

**Lock the hinge angle • hamstrings loaded • pull hard without changing position**

# D. Strap-Assisted Hamstring Curl Iso

## Concept

Use a non-elastic strap or fixed implement to create knee-flexion resistance while the hamstrings are kept at a selected long muscle length.

## Why it belongs

The Standing Glute-Ham emphasizes the hamstrings across the hip. A targeted knee-flexion iso allows the system to address the hamstrings' second major function without replacing the integrated hinge.

## Suggested setup

- Use a prone, seated, or standing configuration where the lower leg can pull against an immovable strap.
- Choose a hip position that keeps the hamstrings meaningfully lengthened.
- Avoid fully locking the knee or forcing aggressive end-range stretch.

## Learn Mode sequence

1. Set the hip position first.
2. Establish a slight knee bend.
3. Route the strap so it resists knee flexion without twisting the knee.
4. Gradually attempt to curl the heel toward the body.
5. Maintain pelvis and hip position while increasing force.
6. Keep the contraction muscular rather than cramp-dominant.

## Workout-mode cues

**Long hamstring position • stable pelvis • curl the heel into the strap**

## Family programming role

### Beginner
- Supported Standing Glute-Ham
- Integrated Standing Glute-Ham once competency is established

### Intermediate
- Integrated Standing Glute-Ham
- Strap-Assisted Targeted Hamstring Hinge
- optional short hamstring-curl iso exposures

### Advanced
- longer accumulated Standing Glute-Ham work;
- high-intent strap hinge work;
- long-length knee-flexion isometrics;
- Extreme Slow hinge variations;
- later pairing with dynamic hinge / rebound / force-absorption work when the broader program supports it.

## Provenance notes

### Schroeder / EVO Original
- Standing Glute-Ham / Standing Hamstring as a core position
- active pulling into a deeper position
- hamstring / glute opposition while moving deeper

### Schroeder-Derived
- supported teaching version
- toe-elevated heel-biased variant
- specific cueing refinements from later coaches and former users

### Evidence-Based Extension
- fixed-strap targeted hinge
- long-length strap hamstring-curl iso

## Open research questions

1. Determine whether heels-together / toes-out should be a default historical geometry or presented only as one documented version.
2. Clarify how much deliberate spinal relaxation Schroeder intended versus later coaching exaggerations of a rounded upper back.
3. Validate the best strap geometry for long-length knee-flexion work.
4. Determine whether a separate elevated-deadlift Extreme Iso belongs in this family or should remain an advanced force-production method outside the core exercise library.

## Key source trail

- Archived Schroeder / EVO material listing Standing Glute-Ham as a core Iso-Extreme and describing active pulling into position.
- Later coaching summaries describing feet-close / toes-out geometry, soft knees, hip hinge, relaxed upper back, and downward-pulling intent.
- Former EvoCode users describing heel-biased and toe-elevated variants as later coaching modifications rather than universal rules.
