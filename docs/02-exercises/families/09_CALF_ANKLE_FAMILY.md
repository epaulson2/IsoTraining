# Calf / Ankle Family

## Status

**Validated family draft — evidence-based extension.**

## Family role

This family adds ankle plantar-flexor work, which is not prominent in the classic EVO sequence but is useful for lower-leg strength, tendon loading and long-muscle-length isometric training.

## Primary versions

### A. Straight-Knee Calf Iso
- **Type:** Integrated / targeted hybrid
- **Provenance:** Evidence-based extension
- **Primary emphasis:** Gastrocnemius

### B. Bent-Knee Calf Iso
- **Type:** Integrated / targeted hybrid
- **Provenance:** Evidence-based extension
- **Primary emphasis:** Soleus

### C. Strap-Assisted Calf Iso
- **Type:** Targeted
- **Provenance:** Evidence-based extension
- **Use:** Precise force intent and reduced balance limitation

## Straight-Knee Calf Iso

### Setup

- Stand on a step or firm elevation with the forefoot supported.
- Keep the knee straight but not hyperextended.
- Allow the heel to lower into a tolerable dorsiflexed position.
- Maintain tripod forefoot pressure rather than rolling to the inside or outside edge.
- Use hand support during learning so balance does not limit calf tension.

### Active intent

Attempt to drive the forefoot down and raise the heel while the position remains essentially fixed. Do not simply hang at the bottom on the Achilles/calf complex.

### Learn Mode

1. Establish secure forefoot support.
2. Find neutral foot alignment.
3. Lower the heel gradually to a muscularly loaded position.
4. Maintain a straight but soft knee.
5. Learn low-level plantar-flexion intent.
6. Increase force without rolling the ankle.
7. Hold 8–15 seconds cleanly.
8. Rise or step off under control.

### Workout-mode cues

**Heel low • foot centered • drive the forefoot down**

## Bent-Knee Calf Iso

### Setup

- Use the same secure forefoot support.
- Bend the knee enough to reduce gastrocnemius contribution and emphasize the soleus.
- Maintain heel-down dorsiflexion without collapsing the arch.
- Keep the heel tracking over the forefoot.

### Active intent

Attempt to plantar-flex and raise the heel while the joint angle remains fixed.

### Learn Mode

1. Set forefoot support.
2. Bend knee to a repeatable angle.
3. Lower heel gradually.
4. Keep arch and heel aligned.
5. Build plantar-flexion force.
6. Hold 8–15 seconds with no ankle roll.

### Common compensations

- foot rolling inward or outward;
- knee drifting unpredictably;
- bouncing into end range;
- passive Achilles hanging;
- cramping from excessive initial intensity.

## Strap-Assisted Variant

A non-elastic strap may be used to stabilize the body or provide an immovable plantar-flexion resistance vector. The strap should make the target contraction easier to isolate, not create awkward compression across the foot or ankle.

## Rationale

The isometric literature supports manipulating muscle length and intensity, and higher-intensity loading is relevant to tendon adaptations. These calf exercises are therefore useful extensions, but they are not represented as Schroeder-original positions.
