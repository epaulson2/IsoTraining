# Hip Flexor / Long-Split Family

## Status

**Validated family draft — evidence-based extension built from split-stance principles.**

## Family role

This family emphasizes controlled hip extension on the trailing leg while preserving pelvis and trunk organization. It is intentionally distinct from the core Extreme Lunge, where the front leg carries more of the primary training demand.

## Primary versions

### A. Supported Long-Split Hip-Flexor Iso
- **Type:** Integrated
- **Provenance:** Evidence-based extension
- **Use:** Learn Mode, positional control and long-length hip-flexor loading

### B. Rear-Leg Active Split Iso
- **Type:** Integrated / targeted hybrid
- **Provenance:** Schroeder-derived extension
- **Use:** Greater rear-leg contribution while maintaining the split stance

### C. Strap Hip-Flexor Iso
- **Type:** Targeted
- **Provenance:** Evidence-based extension
- **Use:** Precise high-intent hip-flexor loading at a selected angle

## Supported Long-Split Hip-Flexor Iso

### Setup

- Use a split stance long enough to create meaningful hip extension on the trailing side.
- Use hand support so balance is not the limiting factor.
- Keep the pelvis approximately square.
- Maintain rib cage over pelvis rather than gaining apparent hip extension through lumbar arching.
- Rear knee may be straight or slightly bent depending on the selected variant and comfort.

### Active intent

Do not simply hang in a hip-flexor stretch. Actively maintain posterior pelvic/trunk control while the rear leg contributes force into the floor. The trainee may imagine trying to drag the rear foot forward without allowing it to move, creating active hip-flexor tension at long length.

### Learn Mode

1. Build a stable split stance with support.
2. Square pelvis and rib cage.
3. Find rear-hip extension without lumbar compensation.
4. Establish front- and rear-foot pressure.
5. Learn gentle rear-foot forward-drag intent.
6. Maintain glute/trunk co-contraction so the pelvis does not spill forward.
7. Increase force while keeping the position visually stable.
8. Hold 8–15 seconds cleanly.
9. Shorten the stance before standing fully upright.

### Common compensations

- lumbar extension masquerading as hip extension;
- pelvis rotating open;
- rear foot becoming passive;
- anterior hip pinching;
- chasing maximal split depth.

### Workout-mode cues

**Ribs over pelvis • rear hip long • drag rear foot forward without moving**

---

## Strap Hip-Flexor Iso

Use a secure strap arrangement to resist hip flexion while the hip starts from an extended or neutral-to-extended position. The setup must not place unsafe pressure over the knee, groin or abdomen.

### Workout-mode cues

**Pelvis fixed • hip starts long • drive thigh forward into the strap**

## Rationale

This family gives the hip flexors an active long-length role rather than treating them only as tissues to stretch. It complements, rather than replaces, the core lunge family.
