# Anterior Chain / Quadriceps Family

## Status

**Post-audit validated family draft.** This file was renamed conceptually from “Quadriceps Long-Length” because the historical standing straight-leg raise is not a globally long-length quadriceps position.

## Family role

This family contains three different anterior-chain purposes that should not be conflated:

1. **Standing Straight-Leg Raise Iso** — historical EVO-associated anterior-chain position emphasizing hip-flexor effort plus active knee extension.
2. **Supported Sissy-Squat Iso** — the primary long-length quadriceps position in this family.
3. **Strap Knee-Extension Iso** — targeted quadriceps force at a deliberately selected knee angle.

## A. Standing Straight-Leg Raise Iso

- **Type:** Targeted / integrated hybrid
- **Provenance:** Schroeder / EVO core-associated
- **Programming tier:** B — Secondary / Developmental

### Historical anchor

Surviving Schroeder-associated material describes the standing straight-leg raise as a quadriceps/anterior-chain Iso-Extreme and emphasizes a roughly 90-degree relationship between the raised thigh and torso.

### Biomechanical correction

Do **not** describe this as a “long-length quadriceps” exercise. Hip flexion shortens rectus femoris proximally, while knee extension shortens the quadriceps across the knee. Its value is better understood as active anterior-chain positional control: maintaining hip flexion while aggressively preserving knee extension and trunk position.

### Setup

- Stand tall with support available for balance.
- Lift one leg in front while keeping the knee extended or nearly extended.
- Bring the thigh toward roughly perpendicular to the torso only if that range can be maintained without lumbar rounding, posterior pelvic collapse or hip pinching.
- Keep the stance leg stable and pelvis level.
- Avoid leaning backward to manufacture apparent hip-flexion range.

### Active intent

Actively maintain knee extension and continue trying to raise the thigh farther while the trunk remains still. The effort should feel muscular through the hip-flexor/anterior-thigh complex rather than like passive compression in the front of the hip.

### Learn Mode

1. Establish single-leg standing balance with hand support.
2. Lock in a tall trunk and level pelvis.
3. Raise the working leg gradually.
4. Maintain active knee extension.
5. Find the highest clean position without leaning back.
6. Create low-level upward-pull intent through the thigh.
7. Add strong quadriceps tension to keep the knee straight.
8. Hold 8–15 seconds cleanly.
9. Lower under control.

### Workout-mode cues

**Stand tall • knee long • pull the thigh higher without leaning**

## B. Supported Sissy-Squat Iso

- **Type:** Integrated
- **Provenance:** Schroeder-derived / later associated use
- **Programming tier:** A — Programming Core

### Purpose

Provide the clearest long-length quadriceps stimulus in this family by combining substantial knee flexion with a relatively extended hip position while support removes balance as the primary limiter.

### Setup

- Use stable hand support.
- Rise onto the forefeet as needed and allow the knees to travel forward while the body remains in a controlled line.
- Descend only to a range that creates strong quadriceps loading without knee pain or anterior-hip pinching.
- Keep hips from folding backward into a normal squat pattern.
- Maintain a controlled rib-cage/pelvis relationship rather than arching aggressively to chase depth.

### Active intent

Maintain the long quadriceps position while actively attempting to extend the knees without visibly rising. The body should remain organized rather than hanging passively on the knee structures.

### Learn Mode

1. Establish secure support.
2. Learn the forefoot-supported knee-travel pattern at shallow depth.
3. Keep torso and thighs organized as one controlled line.
4. Descend gradually into greater knee flexion.
5. Find a strongly muscular but pain-free position.
6. Build low-level knee-extension force.
7. Increase extension intent without rising or folding at the hips.
8. Hold 6–10 seconds cleanly.
9. Use support to exit smoothly.

### Workout-mode cues

**Stay long • knees forward • extend without rising**

## C. Strap Knee-Extension Iso

- **Type:** Targeted
- **Provenance:** Evidence-Based Extension
- **Programming tier:** B — Secondary / Developmental

### Purpose

Create precise knee-extension force at a selected joint angle. This is useful when the program calls for targeted quadriceps loading without the stabilization demands of the sissy squat, wall squat or single-leg squat.

### Setup

- Secure the strap so knee extension is resisted without pressure across sensitive structures.
- Stabilize the thigh and pelvis so the effort does not become hip flexion or trunk movement.
- Select the knee angle according to the desired training emphasis rather than assuming one universal position.
- Do not force painful end-range knee flexion.

### Active intent

Attempt to extend the knee powerfully against the immovable strap while keeping the thigh, pelvis and selected knee angle stable.

### Workout-mode cues

**Thigh fixed • knee angle fixed • extend hard into the strap**

## Programming decision after audit

- **Core:** Supported Sissy-Squat Iso
- **Secondary:** Standing Straight-Leg Raise Iso; Strap Knee-Extension Iso

The historical straight-leg raise remains in the library because of its EVO lineage, but it should not be used as the scientific justification for long-length quadriceps training.
